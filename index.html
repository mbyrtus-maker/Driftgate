Driftgate

Three powers, an alien Brood, fifteen sectors and one closed economy. Every plate, coil and crystal was mined from a rock that already existed. Mine it, forge it, bolt it to your ship, and fight over the stations and outposts that make the best parts possible.

Instruments

The amber ring ahead of you is where your guns point. Put it on the target's diamond lead marker to hit a moving ship. The green circle shows which way you are actually drifting. The radar is a disc around your ship: blips sit on stalks above or below the disc to show height.

Missiles. Target a hostile and keep it inside the dashed lock ring around your gun reticle. A diamond closes in on the target; when it turns red and reads LOCKED, press C. Missiles home on the target but turn slower than ships, so hard turns and flares can beat them. Red arrows on the ring around the centre point to hostiles off-screen; a blinking warning means someone is locking you or a missile is inbound.

Frigates claim territory. A frigate holds any system where no rival frigate is present and no hostile station stands: it claims the system for its side, mines it into your vault (or its faction's stores) and takes the outpost after a minute. Facing a hostile faction depot, an automatic battery closes to range and shells it; when the depot's hull breaks, the frigate captures it rather than destroying it. Bloom Nodes are burned out, and faction capitals only go dark.

Distress calls. When a friendly station, depot or outpost is attacked anywhere in the galaxy, it radios every pilot of its faction: a two-tone chirp and a message saying who is under attack, where and how many jumps away. The call goes into the Friendly ops list, and O sets course to it.

Magazines, not ammunition. Missiles, torpedoes and swarm pods never need building or loading. Each hull carries a set number of shots (more with Weapons Mk). Once a magazine is below full, a reload countdown runs (missiles 20 s, torpedoes 35 s, swarm pods 30 s at Payload Bay Mk I) and then it refills completely; the HUD shows the count and the countdown. Each Payload Bay upgrade in the Shipyard cuts reload times by 15%, down to a floor of 10 s for missiles, 20 s for torpedoes and 15 s for swarm pods. Only Aurora tuning on the bay (made with deep-space Aurorite) goes below the floor: each tuning level takes 2 s off every reload (up to 4 s). You can target hostile stations, depots and outposts with T, Q or a click as well as ships and frigates; only torpedoes and capital guns hurt station armour.

Hulls. Every pilot starts in a Light hull: a Light Fighter, Light Miner or Light Hauler. You can switch between those three starter hulls for free in the Shipyard at your faction's home station. Bigger hulls are built in a core station's Shipyard from materials (plates, conduit, coils and, for the heaviest, Crystal Lattice) plus a small fee; the old frame's materials come back to you. Miners and haulers come in Light, Medium and Heavy: more mining lasers, more guard drones and bigger holds as they grow. Load Guard Drones into your drone bay in the Shipyard; they launch with you and defend your ship. Light fighters are the fastest, accelerate hardest, turn tightest, burn the least energy on afterburner and shake missiles best (flares work more often), but carry small racks and thin armour. Medium fighters are nearly as quick and nimble with heavier guns: both can out-run and out-turn heavies and bombers, and staying out of a heavy's nose is how you survive its swarm. Heavy fighters trade speed for armour, bigger guns and a larger missile rack. Bombers are slow and tough and carry Siege Torpedoes: lock an outpost (it takes longer), launch, and each torpedo hits it three times as hard as a missile. Torpedoes are slow and turn badly, but fighter guns can't touch one once it's away: only station, depot and frigate point-defence turrets can shoot them down. Stop the bombers before they launch, and escort your own so they get their shots off (enemy torpedoes show a yellow TORPEDO marker). Change hull at your faction's main station.

Kinetic vs laser. Both guns share your Weapons upgrade. Kinetic cannons (1) fire slugs that take time to arrive, so aim at the diamond lead marker; they sip energy, reach about 950 m and hit hull 15% harder. Lasers (2) hit instantly wherever your nose points, no lead needed, but drain about three times the energy, reach only 820 m and are 25% stronger against shields and weaker against hull. The reticle becomes a diamond in laser mode and turns red when the beam is on a target. A good pattern: strip shields with lasers, then finish the hull with kinetics.

Stations and depots. Faction stations and depots carry shields and turrets (6 on a main station, 4 on a depot) that fire on hostiles within about 1.3–1.5 km and shoot down torpedoes. Knock a station's shield down and its turrets go offline for 20 seconds; the station itself cannot be destroyed.

The Brood. A fourth power grown, not built, out of The Bloom (top of the map). Their ships are tough and their hulls regrow out of combat. Brood swarms take outposts, plant Bloom Nodes and try to destroy stations. Every faction hunts them first, then goes back to fighting each other.

Stations can fall, but only to torpedoes. Guns and missiles cannot hurt station or depot armour; Bomber torpedoes can (five times their normal damage). Once the shield breaks the turrets go dark and the hull takes the hits. Depots at zero hull are destroyed and their frames scatter as debris; main stations and the Bloom Heart only go dark for a while. Station point defence only reaches about 700 m, so launch salvos. Siege fleets bring bombers while their fighters picket the station.

Gunboats. A two-seat hull from the Shipyard (16 plates, 10 conduit, 5 coils, 1 lattice). The pilot flies on twin forward cannons and missiles. A second pilot can pull alongside a friendly gunboat at matched speed and press E to take the rear turret: the mouse aims, click or Space fires, C launches a missile from the shared rack, and E climbs back out to your own ship. When nobody is in the turret, an AI gunner tracks and fires at whatever comes near.

Comms. Press I to pick a friendly: the first press is you, then nearby friendlies. Choose 1 to have up to four nearby friendly fighters guard that ship, or 2 to have them escort it, for two minutes. Choose 3 to ask your faction to build: a capital shipyard in your home system, or a depot where you are anywhere else. It draws on the faction's stores, and the reply says what is missing.

Commanding a frigate. Fly up to one of your frigates and press E to dock: you step onto its bridge. Walk with WASD (Shift to hurry); click the view to look around with the mouse (Esc releases it), and press E at a console. The helm and weapons consoles sit just behind the forward window, the strategic table is in the middle, and there are windows port and starboard. The command chair on the raised dais at the back (E) brings up every console in one place: strategic, helm, weapons, engineering and comms (1 to 5 switch tabs). E or Esc stands you up again. The Comms console by the starboard window sends guard and escort orders to nearby friendly fighters and asks your faction to build a depot or a shipyard. Strategic shows the sector live: click space to move there, click a hostile to give it to the capital battery. Navigation sets throttle and runs the jump drive: frigates need no gates, the drive spins up (20 s at normal power) and jumps to a neighbouring system from anywhere, or follows a plotted course. Weapons sets the battery (automatic, a chosen target, or hold fire), point defence priorities, and whether the two Pickets escort or attack. Engineering splits 12 units of power between engines, shields, the battery and point defence. The hatch at the back returns you to your fighter.

Playing with friends. Up to four pilots can share a galaxy: on the start screen everyone types a callsign and the same galaxy code. The first pilot in hosts; the others join them. Each of you picks your own faction, so you can fly together or fight each other. Press Enter in flight to message everyone. Every pilot keeps a copy of the latest save, so anyone can host the next session, and if the host leaves mid-game another pilot takes over within a few seconds. While nobody is online the galaxy waits, frozen. Capital shipyards and frigates are single-player only for now; outposts you claim and depots you build belong to your faction.

Fighter screens. Friendly fighter wings in a frigate's system form up around it (up to three wings) and screen it: they push out to catch bombers before they can launch, then take on anything else closing in. Once a torpedo is away, the screen can't stop it; only the frigate's point defence can. Your own frigates are screened by your faction's wings.

Winning the galaxy. A side that controls 8 systems for 30 minutes wins. The nine quiet systems (thin fields, little traffic) don't raise that bar, but holding one with a depot or a frigate counts toward it. The galaxy resets, and the winner starts the next one with a finished capital shipyard and 40% richer stores; if it was your faction, you start with 4,000 extra credits and a crate of parts.

Capital ships. At your faction's main station you can build a Capital Shipyard (4 Depot Frames, 4 Capital Keels, 4 Crystal Lattice, 40 plates and 5,000 cr). It commissions Frigates (6 Keels, 6 Lattice, 2 Resonance Cores, 10 conduit, 2 Picket Kits, 4 missiles). Order your frigates from the galaxy map. A frigate holds any system with no station unless another frigate is there: it mines the system for you and takes its outpost after about a minute. Each flies with two Pickets, small fighters with two missiles each. Point-defence turrets shred fighters and torpedoes; a heavy capital battery cracks station hulls and other frigates. Fighter guns and missiles only drain a frigate's shield; torpedoes (four times damage) and capital guns break the hull. AI factions build yards and frigates too, and the Brood grows Leviathans in The Bloom. The galaxy's asteroid fields are richer to feed all this (Capital Keels need lots of Iridium).

Aurorite. A fifth ore that exists only in the three deep-space sectors (Ashen Expanse, Glass Shallows, Lantern Deep), glowing teal in the rock. It never re-forms anywhere else: dust from wrecks drifts back toward deep space. Fabricate it into Aurora Tuning (2 Aurorite + 1 Flux Coil) and attune any component in the Shipyard: each level adds +6% to that component (two levels max, 1 then 2 tunings). Whoever holds depots in deep space mines Aurorite, and their fighters start showing up tuned.

Missiles and swarms. Missiles and swarm micro-missiles hit 1.8× harder once a target's shield is down, so break shields with guns and finish with missiles. Heavy Fighters and Bombers carry a swarm launcher with 2 pods (fire with 3): eight micro-missiles fan out at your target and anything flying near it. Flares fool only about half of them. AI heavy fighters and bombers sometimes carry one too.

Constructors. Factions send escorted constructor convoys to build depots in sectors without a station, and to rebuild lost frontier depots. You can too: buy a Constructor hull at a core station, fabricate 2 Depot Frames, fly to a sector with no station and press K. Your depot mines that sector for you and defends itself.

Sensors, not omniscience. You only hear about strikes, convoys and battles in sectors your side can see: sectors with a friendly station or depot (plus their neighbours), friendly outposts, and wherever a friendly scout is (plus neighbours). Holding the Hollow Relay taps the whole network. The map dims sectors you cannot see. Scout hulls are the fastest ships, see the whole sector and the sectors next door, but carry light guns and turn slowly.

Wings. Combat ships fly in wings of three or more and share targets. When a wing is cut down to one or two ships, the survivors break off and run for home.

Shields. Hits flare blue on your shield bubble and the screen edges. When the shield collapses it bursts outward, the edges glow red and the shield bar blinks: boost away with Shift. Shields start recharging 2.5 s after the last hit, except in an ion storm.

To jump, line up in front of a gate ring with your nose pointing straight through it (within 20°) and hold steady: the gate counts down 5 seconds, then flings you through. Turn away or fly off and the countdown resets. Autopilot (J) lines up for you. Gates through Hollow Verge charge a toll to whoever holds the Hollow Relay.

The closed economy

Nothing is created. Ferrite, Cuprite, Iridium and Xenocrystal exist as a fixed total mass. Mining moves ore from rocks into holds. Fabrication combines it into plates, conduit, coils, lattices and cores. Upgrades bolt those parts permanently into your ship.

Nothing is destroyed. Wrecked ships spill half their parts as salvage; the rest becomes debris, which drifts between sectors and slowly re-forms into new asteroids. Credits work the same way: every credit you earn left a faction treasury.

Why own an outpost. The three Forges (Kestrel, Shoal, Cinder) are the only places Crystal Lattice and Resonance Cores can be made, and the only docks that fit Mk IV and Mk V parts. The Hollow Relay collects gate tolls and gives early warning of strike groups. The Wrack Salvage Yard harvests debris. Owned outposts also skim ore from their sector's fields.

Claiming. Fabricate a Claim Beacon, fly to an unclaimed or breached outpost, and hold its ring until the claim completes. Hostile outposts must be shot down to zero hull first. Fortify yours with parts, because the factions and the Unbound will come for it.
